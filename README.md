
**[ GENERAL INTELLIGENCE & REASONING ]**
 * **MATHEMATICAL OPERATIONS [3Q]**
   * **Equation Balancing & BODMAS**
     * **Q18:** Sign Substitution to Balance Equation **[ Cal/R | H/75s | Skip ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Attempting to solve the equation linearly from left to right instead of strictly adhering to BODMAS rules, leading to incorrect massive values.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Always test the division sign (÷) first in the options. It must be placed where it perfectly divides the adjacent numbers without leaving a decimal fraction (e.g., checking if 44 ÷ 22 yields an integer).
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Fast divisibility checks and the priority order of BODMAS operations.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Fractional remainders intended to waste calculation time.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Questions where both mathematical signs and actual numbers must be interchanged simultaneously to balance the equation.
     * **Q19:** Decimal BODMAS Calculation **[ Cal/C | M/60s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Misaligning the decimal points during the final addition and subtraction steps after correctly solving the division and multiplication.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Use unit digit approximation or round off to the nearest whole number (e.g., 46 ÷ 23 = 2, 24 x 13 = 312). Adding and subtracting these rough estimates immediately points to the ~257 range.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Speed multiplication of two-digit floating-point numbers.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Options intentionally set to the exact results of miscalculated BODMAS sequences.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Incorporating percentages and square roots alongside decimal operators.
     * **Q20:** Sign Interchange for Equation Balancing **[ Cal/R | M/55s | Rev ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Swapping signs but forgetting to recalculate the entire equation from scratch, or misapplying the multiplication before the new division.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Target the ÷ and x signs for swapping. Swapping ÷ and x changes 64 x 16 to 64 ÷ 16, simplifying a massive multiplier into a highly manageable integer (4), which easily leads to the target 2063.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Visual scanning for 'illegal' operations that yield fractions when integers are required on the RHS.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Sign swaps that result in deep negative numbers.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Cyclical sign swapping (e.g., + becomes -, - becomes x, x becomes +).
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Mathematical Operations**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Strict BODMAS application, decimal approximations, and tactical sign interchange to balance heavy equations [Q18, Q19, Q20].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Number interchange or symbol-to-operator coding (e.g., A means +, B means -).
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Simultaneous sign and number interchanges within the same equation.
     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Intermediate. Requires high calculation speed and mental math, rather than deep theoretical logic.
     5. **Topic Priority Score:** 8/10
     6. **Topic Revision Strategy:** Daily numerical practice of 15-20 simplification equations using a strict countdown timer.
 * **CLASSIFICATION (ODD ONE OUT) [3Q]**
   * **Letter & Number Cluster Classification**
     * **Q11:** Letter Cluster Gaps **[ Log/C | E/20s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Miscounting the alphabetical gap by accidentally including the starting letter in the count.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Map out the numerical place values mentally. AF-CI involves shifts that deviate from the standard +5, +2 gap seen in the other clusters.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Rote memorization of A-Z forward and backward numerical positional values.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Meaningless Vowel/Consonant ratios used as red herrings.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Clusters based on reverse alphabetical pairs (A-Z, B-Y) mixed with +1/-1 shifts.
     * **Q12:** Letter Cluster Increments **[ Log/C | E/20s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Assuming all pairs follow the same increment pattern without checking the final letter of each cluster.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Check the gap between the first and second letter of each option. F(+2)H, C(+2)E, K(+2)M, V(+2)X. Since the internal gaps are identical, check the jump across the hyphen.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Quick identification of uniform arithmetic progressions within letter series.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Prime number place-value gaps.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Four-letter clusters featuring alternating positive and negative alphabetical shifts.
     * **Q17:** Number Pair Multipliers **[ Log/R | M/45s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Wasting time trying to find an addition/subtraction difference instead of immediately checking for a constant fractional multiplier.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Divide the second number by the first. 42.5 ÷ 17 = 2.5. 47.5 ÷ 19 = 2.5. The odd one (29-78.3) will clearly not yield exactly 2.5.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Rapid recognition of x1.5, x2.5, x3.5 multiplication patterns in two-digit numbers.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Square or Cube relationships with a +/- constant.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Number pairs based on algebraic logic like (x^2 + x) or (x^3 - x).
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Classification (Odd One Out)**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Positional arithmetic gaps in letter clusters [Q11, Q12] and fractional multipliers in number pairs [Q17].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Meaning-based word classification or general knowledge grouping (e.g., capitals, currencies).
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Classification based on the sum of digits or sum of alphabetical place values within a cluster.
     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Basic. Relies completely on fast observation and rigid memory of place values and basic multipliers.
     5. **Topic Priority Score:** 9/10
     6. **Topic Revision Strategy:** Pure memorization of multiplication tables up to 30, squares up to 30, and A-Z positions.
 * **SERIES [2Q]**
   * **Alphabet & Number Series**
     * **Q5:** Multi-Variable Letter Series **[ Log/C | M/35s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Looking at the letter cluster as a whole word rather than tracking the individual positions (first letter sequence, second letter sequence) independently.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Map only the first letters: H(+5) = M, M(+5) = R, R(+5) = W, W(+5) = B. The answer must start with R.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Tracking independent arithmetic progressions (+5, -3, -8) across isolated index positions in a string.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Vowel progression sequences.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Alphanumeric series combining letter shifts with squared number increments (e.g., A1, D4, I9).
     * **Q16:** Escalating Number Series **[ Log/R | H/60s | Rev ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Trying to find a single-tier difference and giving up when it fails, instead of checking double-differences or square/cube additions.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Check the gaps: 1, 8, 9, 64. These are alternating cubes and squares: 1^3, 2^3, 3^2, 4^3. The next gap is 5^3 or 5^2.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Identifying sequences built on alternating squares and cubes of consecutive integers.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Fibonacci sequence additions.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Series gaps based on prime number cubes (8, 27, 125, 343).
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Series**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Independent index tracking in letters [Q5] and alternating square/cube gaps in numbers [Q16].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Continuous alphanumeric series or repeating pattern series (e.g., a_bc_ab_c).
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Decimal multiplier series (e.g., x0.5, x1, x1.5, x2).
     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Intermediate. Requires quick transition between addition logic and exponent logic.
     5. **Topic Priority Score:** 7/10
     6. **Topic Revision Strategy:** Practice identifying 2nd and 3rd tier differences in high-escalation number strings.
 * **ALPHABET TEST & WORD FORMATION [2Q]**
   * **Positional Logic & Anagrams**
     * **Q6:** Relative Alphabet Position **[ Cpt/C | E/15s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Counting manually from the ends instead of using positional formulas, eating up valuable time.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** 15th from start is O (15). 14th from end is 27 - 14 = 13th from start, which is M (13). The letter exactly between 13 and 15 is 14 (N).
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** The 27 - n rule for finding the reverse positional value of an English letter.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Reversing the first half of the alphabet.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Finding the exact middle letter after dropping all vowels from the alphabet.
     * **Q7:** Meaningful Word Creation **[ Mem/R | H/45s | Skip ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Wasting 2+ minutes trying to form a word and suffering from "tip of the tongue" syndrome.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Extract the letters quickly: B, E, L, M, O, P, R. Scan for common prefixes/suffixes. Forms "PROBLEM". Middle letter is B. If a word isn't obvious in 15 seconds, skip immediately.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Anagram solving techniques grouping vowels and common consonant blends (pr, bl, st).
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Forming multiple valid words, triggering a "More than one word" option trap.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Questions asking for the 3rd letter of a word formed by alternating letters of a given 14-letter word.
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Alphabet Test & Word Formation**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Reverse alphabetical positioning [Q6] and single-word anagram assembly [Q7].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Dictionary/Alphabetical ordering of 5 different words.
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Conditional alphabet series (e.g., "If every alternate letter is dropped...").
     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Basic. The anagram question relies on inherent vocabulary; don't over-study it.
     5. **Topic Priority Score:** 6/10
     6. **Topic Revision Strategy:** Master the exact numeric positions of A-Z to calculate midpoints instantly.
 * **ORDER & RANKING [2Q]**
   * **Linear Position & Age Comparison**
     * **Q2:** Relative Position in Row **[ Cpt/C | M/30s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Adding or subtracting 1 incorrectly when converting from a Right-end position to a Left-end position.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Ravi is 20th from Right. In a row of 50, Ravi's position from Left = (50 - 20) + 1 = 31st. Rahul is 15th to the left of Ravi: 31 - 15 = 16th from Left.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** The Total = (Left + Right) - 1 formula for single entities, and shifting logic for relative entities.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Overlapping ranks where total students are fewer than the sum of left and right positions.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Rank interchange questions where two people swap seats and a new total must be calculated.
     * **Q4:** Inequality/Age Comparison **[ Log/C | E/25s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Trying to hold the entire comparison in working memory instead of jotting down a quick greater-than/less-than chain.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Build a vertical chain: S > T > P > R > Q > U. The second youngest (second from the bottom) is directly Q.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Transcribing text inequalities directly into unified mathematical chains (A > B > C).
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Statements defining who is *not* the oldest or youngest, requiring elimination.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Combining age ranking with a second variable like weight or height.
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Order & Ranking**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Single-variable linear equations [Q2] and direct inequality chaining [Q4].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Overlapping/Minimum total cases in row rankings.
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Position changes (interchanging seats) requiring a recount of the total line.
     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Basic. Highly scoring and reliable.
     5. **Topic Priority Score:** 9/10
     6. **Topic Revision Strategy:** Practice rapid translation of "A is left of B" into visual line segments.
 * **BLOOD RELATIONS [2Q]**
   * **Family Tree & Data Sufficiency**
     * **Q9:** Family Member Counting **[ Log/R | M/40s | Rev ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Assuming every listed relationship represents a distinct, separate individual, leading to an artificially inflated maximum count (e.g., 18).
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Optimize the roles. A person can simultaneously be a father, a son, and a nephew. Overlap roles across 3 generations to find the absolute minimum viable family tree (10 members).
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Constructing multi-generational family trees where one node satisfies multiple relation tags.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Maximum member counts (simple addition).
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Coded blood relations (A # B means A is father of B) nested inside a minimum member puzzle.
     * **Q10:** Blood Relation Data Sufficiency **[ Log/C | M/30s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Automatically assuming Bipin's father's only daughter is Bipin's sister, forgetting that Bipin could potentially be a female name in logical reasoning (though usually male, gender shouldn't be assumed without data).
       > **Golden Shortcut:** St. I gives Mahesh = Devika's son. St. II gives Devika = Bipin's sister. Combining them: Mahesh is Bipin's nephew. Both are strictly required.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Data Sufficiency protocol: always test Statement I alone, then Statement II alone, *before* combining.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** "Only child" vs "Only daughter" distinctions.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Introducing a third statement to evaluate combination redundancies (I+II vs II+III).
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Blood Relations**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Role overlapping for minimum counts [Q9] and evaluating statement dependency [Q10].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Pointing to a photograph scenarios.
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Deep coded inequalities (P $ Q % R) determining gender and relation simultaneously.
     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Intermediate. Focus heavily on not assuming genders based on names.
     5. **Topic Priority Score:** 8/10
     6. **Topic Revision Strategy:** Practice drawing standard generation trees (+ for male, - for female, = for spouse).

     * **Q13:** Mirror Image **[ Out/C | E/10s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Picking a water image (top/bottom flip) instead of a mirror image (left/right flip) if rushing.
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Identify one asymmetrical feature on the extreme left of the original figure; locate it on the extreme right of the correct option. Ignore the rest of the drawing.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Lateral inversion rules for complex geometric shapes and diagonal lines.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Rotated figures (90 or 180 degrees) acting as traps.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Mirror image of an alphanumeric clock face.
     * **Q14:** Embedded Figure **[ Out/C | E/15s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Selecting an option where the embedded shape exists but is scaled incorrectly or rotated (unless rotation is explicitly permitted).
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Focus solely on the intersection nodes and angles of the target figure "X". Scan the options specifically for that unique angle geometry, not the lines themselves.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** Figure masking and hidden geometry extraction.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** Figures embedded with slight dimensional distortions.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** Finding an embedded figure within a highly complex 3D isometric drawing.
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Non-Verbal Reasoning**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Lateral inversion [Q13] and shape extraction [Q14].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Paper folding/cutting, pattern completion.

     4. **Recommended Study Depth:** Basic. Pure visual observation required.

 * **SYLLOGISM [1Q]**
   * **Standard Categorical Logic**
     * **Q3:** 3-Statement Deductions **[ Log/C | M/35s | Solve ]**
       > **Silly Mistake Trap:** Treating "Some A are B" as implying "Some A are NOT B". In pure syllogism, "some" does not limit the possibility of "all".
       > **Golden Shortcut:** Use standard Venn Diagrams. All Ducks in Cats; All Cats in Lions; Some Lions intersect Jungles. Conclusion I (No lions are cats) is visibly false as the Cats circle is inside Lions. Conclusion II is false. Conclusion III (All ducks are lions) is visibly true.
       > 
       * **What EXACTLY to study:** 100/50 rule or pure Venn diagram intersection logic.
       * **Distractor Concepts to Study:** "Possibility" conclusions.
       * **Future Twist Prediction:** "Only a few" and "None but" statement variants.
   * **CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Syllogism**
     1. **Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested:** Basic overlapping and nested subset logic [Q3].
     2. **Micro Concepts Not Tested:** Reverse syllogism (finding statements from given conclusions).
     3. **Likely Next-Year Questions:** Statements utilizing "At least", "Generally", or "Only a few" terminology.
     4. **Recommended Study Depth: Basic to Intermediate.
Topic Priority Score: 9/10
Topic Revision Strategy: Master Venn diagrams for "All", "Some", and "No" configurations.
DIRECTION & DISTANCE [1Q]
Cardinal Tracing
Q8: Sun-Oriented Routing [ App/C | M/35s | Solve ]
Silly Mistake Trap: Failing to register "morning" implies the Sun is strictly in the East, causing a complete misalignment of the entire spatial map.
Golden Shortcut: Translate "towards the sun" immediately to "East". Draw vector lines: E(10) -> S(10) -> W(10) -> N(10). The person essentially walked a 10x10 square back to the origin, then E(20), then N(20). Current position is 20m East from start (actually check vector math closely here).
What EXACTLY to study: Shadow casting (morning vs evening) and vector cancellation (N cancels S, E cancels W).
Distractor Concepts to Study: Pythagoras theorem hypotenuse calculations (not needed here but common).
Future Twist Prediction: Final position calculation requiring Pythagorean theorem for shortest distance.
CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Direction & Distance
Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested: Environmental orientation (Sun/East) and right/left orthogonal turns [Q8].  
Micro Concepts Not Tested: Clockwise/Anti-clockwise angular rotations (e.g., turning 135 degrees).
Likely Next-Year Questions: Questions involving shadows cast to the left/right of a person at sunrise/sunset.
Recommended Study Depth: Basic.
Topic Priority Score: 8/10
Topic Revision Strategy: Use NESW vector cancellation math instead of drawing lines if the puzzle is just vertical/horizontal shifts.
DICE [1Q]
Rotational Logic
Q15: 3-Position Multi-face Cube [ App/C | E/15s | Solve ]
Silly Mistake Trap: Attempting to build a full 3D mental model of the unfolded dice rather than using the one-common-face rule.
Golden Shortcut: Find two dice positions that share exactly ONE symbol. From that common symbol, read the faces in clockwise order. The faces will directly map to their opposites.
What EXACTLY to study: The single-common-face and double-common-face elimination rules for standard and non-standard dice.
Distractor Concepts to Study: Standard dice rules (opposite faces sum to 7) which don't apply to symbol/pattern dice.
Future Twist Prediction: Open/unfolded dice nets where the candidate must identify the valid folded 3D cube.
CONCEPT FAMILY SUMMARY: Dice
Core Concepts Repeatedly Tested: Multi-position cross-referencing to find opposite faces [Q15].  
Micro Concepts Not Tested: Unfolding a dice into a 2D net.
Likely Next-Year Questions: "Which of the following dice cannot be formed from the given net?"
Recommended Study Depth: Basic. One simple rule solves 95% of these questions.
Topic Priority Score: 9/10
Topic Revision Strategy: Memorize the clockwise rotation alignment trick.
